# 7 Step Redox Balancing

## 1. Assign oxidation numbers and separate the reaction

Identify which atoms change their oxidation states. Split the reaction into two half-reactions:
- **Oxidation half-reaction**: The species that loses electrons (oxidation number increases).
- **Reduction half-reaction**: The species that gains electrons (oxidation number decreases).


## 2. Balance all elements except Hydrogen and Oxygen

Add coefficients to ensure the number of non-H and non-O atoms is equal on both sides of each half-reaction.


## 3. Balance Oxygen atoms by adding H2O

For every missing oxygen atom on one side of the equation, add one water molecule $H_{2}O$ to the opposite side.


## 4. Balance Hydrogen atoms by adding hydrogen ions

For every missing hydrogen atom, add one hydrogen ion $H^+$ to the opposite side.


## 5. Balance the charges by adding electrons

Count the total overall charge on the reactant side and the product side for each half-reaction. Add electrons $e^-$ to the more positive side so that the net charge matches the other side.
- **Oxidation**: Electrons are added to the product side.
- **Reduction**: Electrons are added to the reactant side.


## 6. Equalize the electrons and combine the reactions

The number of electrons lost in oxidation must equal the number of electrons gained in reduction. If they do not match, multiply one or both half-reactions by a whole number to make the electron counts equal. Then, add the two half-reactions together and cancel out anything that appears on both sides (including all electrons).


## 7. (For Basic Solutions Only) Neutralize hydrogen ions with hydroxide

If the reaction takes place in a basic medium instead of an acidic medium:
1. Add an equal number of hydroxide ions $OH^-$ to both sides of the final equation to match the number of $H+$ ions.
2. Combine the $H^+$ and $OH^-$ ions on the same side to form water $H_{2}O$.
3. Cancel out any duplicate $H_{2}O$ molecules spanning across the reactant and product sides.



## Examples

### Example 1 (Acidic Medium)

Given :
$$
MnO_{4}^- + Fe^{2+} \to Mn^{2+} + Fe^{3+}
$$

##### 1. Split into Half-Reactions
- $Fe^{2+} \to Fe^{3+}$ (Oxidation: $Fe$ goes from $+2$ to $+3$)
- $MnO_{4}^- \to Mn^{2+}$ (Reduction: $Mn$ goes from $7+$ to $+2$)

> [!important] The charge of Manganese in Permanganate $MnO_{4}^-$
> Permanganate holds $-1$ charge; since each Oxygen holds $-2$ charge, four Oxygen totals $-8$ charge. Manganese in the polyatomic ion must hold $7+$ charge in order to produce net $-1$ charge.

##### 2. Balance Non-H and Non-O Atoms
Both $Fe$ and $Mn$ are already balanced.

##### 3. Balance Oxygen
- $Fe^{2+} \to Fe^{3+}$ (No change)
- $MnO_{4}^- \to Mn^{2+} + 4H_{2}O$ (Added $4H_{2}O$ to balance $O_{4}$)

##### 4. Balance Hydrogen
- $Fe^{2+} \to Fe^{3+}$ (No change)
- $8H^+ + MnO_{4}^- \to Mn^{2+} + 4H_{2}O$ (Added $8H^+$ to balance $4H_{2}$)

##### 5. Balance Charge
- $Fe^{2+} \to Fe^{3+} + 1e^-$ (Right charge was $3+$, so add $1e^-$ to make it equal to $2+$ on the left)
- $5e^- + 8H^+ + MnO_{4}^- \to Mn^{2+} + 4H_{2}O$ (Left charge was $+7$, so add $5e^-$ to make it equal to $2+$ on the right)

> [!important] Placement of electrons
> - Oxidation means it loses electrons. Electrons are placed on the **right**.
> - Reduction means it gains electrons. Electrons are placed on the **left**.

##### 6. Equalize Electrons and Combine

Multiply the entire oxidation half-reaction by $5$ so that both equations involve $5e^-$:

$$
5 \times (Fe^{2+} \to Fe^{3+} + 1e^-) \Longrightarrow 5Fe^{2+} \to 5Fe^{3+} + 5e^-
$$

Now, add them together and cancel out the $5e^-$ on both sides:
$$
\begin{align}
  5Fe^{2+} &\to 5Fe^{3+} + 5e^- \\
  5e^- + 8H^+ + MnO_{4}^- &\to Mn^{2+} + 4H_{2}O \\ \\

  \Longrightarrow 5Fe^{2+} + 8H^+ + MnO_{4}^- &\to 5Fe^{3+} + Mn^{2+} + 4H_{2}O
\end{align}
$$
